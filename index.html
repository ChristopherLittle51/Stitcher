How Stitcher works

This is a puzzle cipher, not serious cryptography. The goal is to reward observation and pattern recognition.

01

Grid geometry

A cipher block has 25 colored cells bounded by 6 horizontal and 6 vertical stitch tracks. Each track has exactly five binary segments.

02

Line message

Each of the six horizontal tracks encodes one 5-bit character. Each of the six vertical tracks does the same.

Stitch present = 0. Empty segment = 1.

03

Color message

Across each of five cell rows, the two hues encode five bits. Down each of five columns, light versus dark encodes another five bits.

Colorblind support: every cell also carries a redundant shape — circle = 00, square = 01, triangle = 10, diamond = 11.

04

Rotation

The colored dot marks the grid's original top-left. Rotate the grid until the dot is upper-left before reading it.

05

Color shift

Blue / orange is shift 0. Moving the hue pair around the 12-step color wheel changes every symbol by that signed shift modulo 32.

The orientation dot uses the grid's bit-0 hue, so its color identifies the shift. A small pip around the dot repeats the same 12-step shift like a clock: 0 at 12 o’clock, positive shifts clockwise, negative shifts counterclockwise.

06

Two messages

The lines and the colors are separate payloads. A grid holds 12 line characters and 10 color characters simultaneously.

5-bit alphabet

Color wheel shifts

The first hue is binary hue bit 0. Its complementary hue, six wheel steps away, is binary hue bit 1.

Colorblind shape key

CircleHue 0 · Light · 00
SquareHue 0 · Dark · 01
TriangleHue 1 · Light · 10
DiamondHue 1 · Dark · 11

Shift marker: the small pip orbiting the orientation dot gives the hue-wheel position without color. Shift 0 = 12 o’clock; each +1 advances one 30° step clockwise.

Hints can be taken in any order. Their costs reflect how much of the puzzle mechanic they reveal.
